      UCLA outlasted Arizona 84-75 in Las Vegas. Coming off of a Final Four
      berth, Arizona once again had high expectations entering the season. The
      Wildcats brought back Ivan Kharchenkov and Mo Krivas, while also adding
      five-star freshman Caleb Holt to the lineup. Unfortunately, this trio’s
      first game together didn’t go as planned.


      Kharchenkov was amazing, piling on an efficient 38 points, the most for an
      Arizona player since Azuolas Tubelis in 2023. However, he received very
      limited offensive help from his teammates. Krivas struggled mightily
      offensively, as UCLA made it a point to swarm him with multiple defenders
      every time he caught the ball in the post. This strategy was very
      effective as it led to multiple jump balls and disrupted possessions.


      Despite UCLA leading for nearly the entire game, Arizona displayed great
      resilience and battled back. With just under a minute remaining, UCLA
      leading by three and Arizona possessing the ball, Krivas caught the ball
      on the left block, looked around, and ultimately passed the ball out of
      bounds, mistaking the Arizona bench for a teammate in the game. This was a
      devastating mistake and effectively ended the Wildcats’ comeback efforts. 


      UCLA was powered by the elite duo of Trent Perry and Jaylen Petty. Perry
      and Petty are going to be one of the best backcourts in the nation as they
      combined for an impressive 34 points, 10 rebounds, and 12 assists. This
      UCLA squad is a feisty, defense-oriented team and made life difficult for
      Arizona all night long. Kusturica didn’t have his best offensive game;
      nevertheless, he impacted the game at a high level through his defense (3
      blocks and 2 steals), spacing, and game-clinching in-bounds pass to Eric
      Dailey Jr. Considering he is only 17 years old, it is unsurprising that
      his offense got off to a slow start. Regardless, it is clear he will be
      very valuable to the Bruins’ success. This UCLA team has a great
      combination of guard play, defense, and depth. The Bruins have established
      themselves as a contender in the Big Ten. 


      Moving forward, there are serious questions regarding Arizona. They
      uncharacteristically got outrebounded by UCLA–something that was an
      overwhelming strength of theirs a year ago–and their offense is clearly
      still a work in progress. For Arizona fans, a potential band-aid to these
      problems could be R.J. Godrey, a fifth-year transfer from Clemson who, as
      of now, has been ruled ineligible by the courts. Godrey would provide
      much-needed strength down low, as well as another scoring threat on
      offense. If Godrey is deemed eligible, Arizona may have the missing piece
      they need; however, that remains to be seen.

      Florida defeated Miami, 77-67 to start the season. Heading into the game,
      I thought Florida was susceptible to a potential upset. Miami made great
      offseason additions, and Jai Lucas is a fantastic coach. Before the game,
      I was hesitant about Florida living up to the hype surrounding this team;
      however, after just one game, all of my concerns about Florida are gone.


      What really stands out to me is how physically imposing they are
      defensively. Miami’s Shelton Henderson and Malik Reneau, two great
      interior scorers, were completely shut down by Florida’s frontline.
      Henderson was forced into out-of-control, contested shots, and Reneau was
      physically overwhelmed.


      Florida’s offense also exhibited positive long-term signs. The Gators shot
      an impressive 11 of 26 from three, a mark they reached just twice all of
      last season. Haugh impressed, finishing with 19 points and 10 rebounds; he
      will be a strong candidate for national player of the year. 


      While Miami came away from this game with a loss, there is no reason for
      panic. Acaden Lewis was brilliant in his first game as a Hurricane,
      scoring 24 points. Although Henderson and Reneau struggled (shooting a
      dreadful 3 for 19), I think that can be attributed more to Florida’s
      exceptional interior defense rather than a sign of things to come. Somto
      Cyril is a mammoth of a person and will be a nightmare for future
      opponents. His dunk against Chinyelu is one of the most ferocious dunks I
      have ever seen. Miami shot a poor 35% from the field and 23.5% from three.
      They will need to make perimeter shots at a higher rate if they want to
      compete for a potential ACC championship, but I have not lost faith in
      them.

College basketball is officially back! After Notre Dame and Villanova kicked off the season in Rome on Sunday, the rest of the country joined the action on Monday. High Point and Liberty got things started at midnight as part of the Field of 68's 24-hour college basketball marathon.

In total, 182 games were played throughout the day, including two highly anticipated matchups between ranked opponents. Of course, no opening day would be complete without a little chaos. This year's biggest shock came courtesy of Oklahoma, which suffered an embarrassing 68–64 home loss to East Texas A&M, a team picked to finish dead last in the Southland Conference.

Porter Moser was already on the hot seat entering the season, and this loss certainly didn't do him any favors. His days in Norman may already be numbered.
