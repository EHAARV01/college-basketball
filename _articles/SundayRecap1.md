As I watch USC close out a much-closer-than-expected victory over Wichita State, the first week of college basketball officially comes to a close. This week was a whirlwind, from a 75-point Illini win on opening night to the second-biggest upset in the last 30 years (I’m looking at you, East Texas A&M). It was the same weird chaos we’ve come to love about college basketball year after year. As the great Jon Rothstein says, “It is only November.”

With that, here are my two headlines for the week.

### **1. Everyone Is Playing Catch-Up**

Florida, Duke, and Illinois are unequivocally the three best teams in the country. The preseason AP poll ranked these teams No. 1, No. 2, and No. 3, respectively. After one week of play, their spots at the top of the sport have only been solidified.

Everyone knew Florida was going to be good. The Gators returned six of their top seven scorers from a team that earned a No. 1 seed a year ago, yet there were still a few whispers about whether they could shoot the ball well enough to reach their full potential. In an emphatic opening statement, they knocked down 11 three-pointers in each of their first two games of the season. That included an impressive 10-point victory over No. 14 Miami, in which they reminded the country that they also have the best frontcourt in the sport.

Duke dismantled both of its Week 1 opponents in routs of 34 and 61 points. Across the first seven minutes of those two games, the Blue Devils outscored their opponents by a combined 37–6. As impressive as that sounds, these results are hardly surprising considering the talent on the roster. Jon Scheyer has assembled possibly the most talented team in the country once again. However, unlike in the past two seasons, it’s still unclear who the best player on the floor will be. This Duke team has the deepest roster in the country and will face mounting pressure to live up to the hype after the way the last two seasons ended. Duke gets an early test on Tuesday when it takes on Michigan State in the Champions Classic.

Illinois cruised to a staggering 75-point win in its first game of the season, the largest margin of victory between two Division I teams in six years. Brad Underwood brought back four of his top six scorers from last year’s Final Four squad, including preseason All-American David Mirković, who will miss at least the first month of the season with an injury. The Illini have also somehow, impossibly, gotten better at offensive rebounding, grabbing a ridiculous 51.4% of their missed shots through two games. The Illini get a true road test in Lubbock on Tuesday, where they’ll face No. 19 Texas Tech.

### **2. UCLA, Virginia, and Memphis Won the Week**

There’s as much buzz around this UCLA team as there has ever been during the Mick Cronin era. The Bruins got a fantastic win on opening night over No. 5 Arizona and led for almost the entire game. The Bruins have clearly returned to being one of the elite defenses in the sport, and they’re just as dangerous on offense. Trent Perry and Jaylen Petty form as dynamic a backcourt as you’ll find in college basketball, and that’s before you even get to freshman phenom Nikola Kusturica. Kusturica is a 6-foot-9, do-everything wing who is projected to go No. 1 overall in the 2028 NBA Draft. After a few subpar years, Cronin clearly has a special feeling about this UCLA squad, even remarking, “I’ve not had a bad day coaching this team.” Even the most die-hard Bruins fans may be skeptical of that. Many projected UCLA to be a fringe Top 25 team. After beating Arizona handily, though, the Bruins have their eyes on a trip to Detroit for another Final Four appearance.

No. 9 Virginia blew out No. 12 Arkansas 91–72 in the first top-12 matchup of the season. All five Virginia starters scored at least 13 points in a game that was nearly out of reach by halftime. When John Calipari spoke at media day a few weeks ago, he so eloquently stated that he must have been on crack for scheduling a team like Virginia. Boy, oh boy, was he right. Virginia put on an offensive clinic, dicing up Arkansas in the pick-and-roll, kicking out for open threes, and pounding the glass for 19 offensive rebounds. Led by Thijs De Ridder and Chance Mallory, Virginia has solidified its place as a top-five team in the country. Despite spending *only* $12 million to $15 million on NIL this offseason, Virginia has fielded a roster more than capable of cutting down the nets in April.

Coming off an incredibly disappointing year in which Memphis finished eighth in the American Conference, many expected the Tigers to get off to another rough start. Nevertheless, after just one week, Memphis has flipped the script, knocking off No. 18 Iowa State and a competitive VCU team. Memphis is now 2–0 and is the only team in the country with both a Top 25 win and a KenPom top-60 win. Jayden Reid, a transfer from Northwestern, has shown he’s a budding superstar, averaging 29 points and four assists in the first week of the season.
